## 1. The verdict

**Go (High confidence).** Gummies sell at your price: Create sells 7 flavours at $37.98 on Amazon and each shows 4K to 10K+ "bought in past month", and your margin is 4.1×.
**The catch:** the category is at its peak, and non-buyers' loudest objections are price versus powder (one YouTube comment has 10,000 likes) and "most gummies have no creatine" (comments under a lab-testing video). Crave and Omni already run ads on lab-tested dose. You win on a different proof, not on "lab tested" alone.
**Next step:** a paused Meta test of 3 concepts at about $30 a day for 7 days; success = cost per purchase under $11.70 (a third of your price).

## 3. The opening

The proven lane is **premium sour gummies at $35 to $38**, sold to people who hate powder. Every leader already says "tastes good" and two say "lab tested". What buyers still complain about is **how many gummies a serving takes** (9.4% of the leader's reviews; Beast Bites needs 5) and **whether the dose is real**. Your angle: a full 5 g serving in fewer gummies, with the batch's lab report on the pack and in the first 3 seconds of the ad. Mark it as needing proof only you have: your gummies per serving and a third-party certificate per batch. Don't copy anyone's "other gummies are fake" claim: it invites the same scrutiny.

## 7. The first test

- **What:** 3 concepts, one ad set each, paused for your review: (1) fewer gummies per 5 g serving, shown against a handful of 5; (2) the batch's lab report on screen in the first 3 seconds; (3) sour bite reaction, taste-first.
- **Where and how much:** Meta, US, about $30 a day per concept for 7 days (about $630).
- **Success:** cost per purchase under $11.70. Hook rate tells you which opening to keep; purchases decide the concept.
- **If it fails:** you learn which proof buyers ignore before you order a second batch.
