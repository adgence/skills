## 1. The verdict

- **Their strategy:** one product, one message stacked four ways ("tastes like candy" + lab-tested dose + money-back guarantee + a 4.7-star headline). They keep ads running for a long time (median 106 days), and since July they've been sending more traffic to listicle and advertorial pages. Right now they're pushing a Christmas price cut through meme-style creator ads.
- **Your opening:** Crave owns *sweet*: "candy" or "crave-worthy" is in the copy of 43 of the 120 ads I read, and none of them says *sour*. But sour isn't empty in the category: **Arrae has run "The Sour Way to Sculpt" on its sour creatine gummy pack for 93 days**, sold to women as toning ("sculpt lean muscle definition without the bloat"). Create sells sour flavor drops too, and Animal Pak launched "Sour Surge" creatine chews for lifters a month ago. So "we're sour" alone makes you the fourth sour brand. What none of them shows in the ads watched is the sour hit itself: Crave can't film a bite reaction for a sweet gummy, Arrae sells sour as a flavor list to women toning, and Animal Pak's is a pack shot. The reaction on camera is yours to take.
- **This week:** shoot the powder-versus-gummy race (their format has run 106+ days in 6 copies), and end it on a sour-face reaction instead of "done first". Run it against a plain taste hook that leads with the reaction, not the word "sour".

- **The anti-powder comparison is their most borrowable format.** Two separate executions (#7 with 6 copies over 106 days, #8 over 148 days) sell the *switch* from powder, not the brand, so neither needs Crave's brand to work. ## 6. Where they're weak

1. **Sour is empty in Crave's account, not in the category.** Their whole taste story is *sweet candy* (43 of 120), so against Crave a sour gummy reads as a different product rather than a cheaper copy. (High.) Against the category it doesn't: Arrae's "The Sour Way to Sculpt" and "3 Sour Flavors, 1 Crave-worthy Creatine Routine" have run 93 days on its sour variety pack, for women who want tone without bloat. Create runs sour flavor drops and Animal Pak sells "Seriously SOUR" chews to lifters, so neither sour nor sour-for-lifters is open. The open ground is the sour hit proven on camera by the reaction. (High for the rivals' ads, read; Medium for the reaction gap: Arrae's sour ads and Animal Pak's show no face reacting, but only one Create sour video was watched)
2. **One persona.** All 10 watched ads star men in their 20s. Only 2 of the 25 creators tagging them look female (from handles and names, Low). They built an after-35 page, but the ad I watched that points to it (#7) shows young men. Women and 35+ are under-served *in creative*. (Medium: 10 of 120 watched)
3. **No TikTok.** No TikTok ads for trycrave.com and no TikTok Shop. Their native-looking creator memes run on Meta only. (High for "none found", MISSING for why)
4. **Comments show objections their ads don't answer.** Of 26 comments counted on the creator post that also runs as their ad (219k views): 8 doubt the dose ("20g is unbelievably unnecessary", 35 likes), 3 joke about kidneys (the top comment has 73 likes), 2 say powder or pills are cheaper and have no sugar, 2 say they'd only buy Creapure. That creator's "20g" bulk claim drew the pile-on. (Medium: one post, 26 comments. Trustpilot had 0 reviews. Their Junip reviews couldn't be read)
5. **Risky claims, don't copy:** the profanity hook (#3); "They say 5g… but lab tests say otherwise" about other brands (needs proof); "stronger, sharper, more energized" and the golf ad's "more power off the tee, sharper focus, faster recovery" (results claims); "sold out six times" scarcity; a landing page named "before-t-listicle" (I didn't read the page, so its claims are unknown); one tagging creator captions the product "Becoming a Addict 💊 … #Drugs" (brand-safety).
