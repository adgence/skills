# The creative strategist's method

How a senior creative strategist thinks, from Motion's creative-strategist skill and its references (performance-metrics, insight-quality, evaluation-framework, concept-standards, brief-template), Motion's competitor-watch, Adgence's creative-strategy notes (Dara Denney, Savannah Sanchez, Barry Hott, Alex Cooper, Meta's diversification guidance) and the tests of this skill. (H) marks a heuristic: a default to use, not a law.

## 1. What each source can and can't say

| Source | Says | Can't say |
|---|---|---|
| The user's own ads (`get_my_ads`, `get_my_ad_breakdown`) | What worked for this brand: spend, CTR, CPA, ROAS, hook and hold rates, trend | Why (that needs the creative watched) |
| Competitors' live ads (`compare_competitor_ads`, `find_competitor_ads`) | What they keep paying to say: run time, copies of a creative, launch pace, landing pages | Their spend, results or targeting. Run time is a signal, not proof: a brand can leave a weak ad on |
| Same-day test groups (`get_brand_ad_profile`) | What they test and which variant survived (once checks are a week apart) | Why it survived (budget can explain it); launch pace (its weekly counts come from the ads it read, longest-running first) |
| Tracking (`whats_new`) | The delta: launches, stops (only when the whole account fits one read), ads crossing 30 and 90 days | Anything before the first check; a change from one check alone |
| Customer comments and reviews | What buyers feel, want, doubt, and the words they use, with counts | How many buyers in the market feel it (a comment sample is the loud and the liked) |
| Organic videos (`find_viral_videos`, `get_creator`) | Which hooks and formats hold attention, with real lift over the creator's median | That they sell |
| The ad library (`my_ad_library`) | Structures already watched, labelled | Performance |

**Copy is not the angle when an account runs one text over many creatives.** RYZE and Everyday Dose put the same line ("powered by functional mushrooms…") over hundreds of different videos (test 1, 23 Nov 2026); the angle lives in the video and the landing page (Everyday Dose sends identical copy to /cortisol, /gut-health and /focus-starter-kit). When most of an account's copy is one text, say so, code angles from the landing paths and the ads watched, and cap any "nobody says X" at Medium.

**Bets versus results (Motion).** Competitor creatives are bets; only own-account numbers are results. Frame market findings as "they're testing this" or "they keep paying for this", never "this works for them". Without an account, label every concept a bet grounded in market signals.

## 2. Performance first, delta over state

- Start from data, not opinions. With an account: pull results before forming a creative view. Without one: competitor and customer signals stand in, labelled.
- **Delta over state.** "Crave launched 14 ads this week, all on one sour-taste claim" is a finding; "Crave runs 120 ads" is a fact sheet. The weekly loop reports change first.
- **Check the season before reading a week.** Black Friday, January, summer sales: when most competitors' launches carry a discount (all three mushroom coffee leaders ran 57 to 75% off in test 1), launches and counts say "promo week", not "new direction", and own CPMs and CPAs are judged against the week after, not a benchmark.
- **Single metrics lie.** Read spend, trend and the goal metric together (Motion's combinations):

| Pattern (own ads) | Means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| High spend, stable or rising efficiency, scaling | The best signal in the account | Protect and iterate (hooks first) |
| High spend, falling efficiency | Scaling past efficiency, or fatigue | Refresh or cap; new angle |
| High hook rate, low hold | The opening promises, the middle loses them | Keep the hook, rework the body |
| High hook, high CPA | Attention without action | Rework body and CTA |
| Low hook rate, low CPA | Converts whoever sees it | New hooks on the same body |
| High CTR, poor conversion | The page doesn't deliver what the ad promised | Fix the page or the promise |
| Strong goal metric, low spend, testing campaign | Early signal, unproven at scale | Graduate to scaling |

Hook rate = 3-second views ÷ impressions (Meta `video_play_actions` with 3 s; TikTok `video_watched_2s`); hold rate = ThruPlays ÷ 3-second views. Judge against the account's own baseline, not industry numbers (H).

**Context before conclusions (Motion evaluation framework):** funnel stage (a retargeting ad that converts is expected), budget starvation (a new ad beside proven winners may never get spend), account stage (learning-phase results are unstable), test fairness (did both ads get a fair chance?).

## 3. The three layers

1. **What's working:** own numbers; without them, the market signals ladder below.
2. **Who it works for:** personas from customer words (identity phrases, situations, "as a…"), and, with an account, age and gender breakdowns (a creative strong with 25 to 34 women and dead with 45+ men is a message signal, not a targeting one).
3. **The pattern:** the taxonomy, one fixed codebook for every ad and comment: angle (the pain or desire × persona), mechanic (reframe, us vs them, social proof, demonstration, myth-bust, objection handling), hook type (Adgence's DNA labels), format, awareness stage (unaware, problem, solution, product, most aware), offer. Which cells are scaling, which are empty, which combinations nobody tried. Coverage comes from the codebook, never from memory of a few ads.

**Market signal ladder (no account), strongest first (H):**

| Signal | Strength |
|---|---|
| One creative with several live copies, launched recently on an old creative | Very high: they're adding spend |
| An angle several competitors converged on and kept 60+ days | High: table stakes, crowded |
| A survivor of a same-day test group | High |
| A buyer theme in 5%+ of a 300+ comment sample, said by buyers (reviews) not browsers | High for an open angle |
| One competitor's 90+ day runner on its own angle | Medium-high: owned |
| 3+ fresh launches on one new theme in a week | Medium-high: a coordinated bet |
| An organic video at 3x+ its creator's median | Medium: proves the hook, not the sale |
| A single new ad | Low |

## 4. The insight bar (Motion insight-quality)

An insight explains what happens in the buyer's mind, not what the ad looks like or what the metric did.
- **Remove the numbers:** does the reasoning still hold? If not, it's a recap.
- **Brand specificity:** could it apply to any brand in the category? Then it's not one.
- **Hindsight:** obvious once said, not before.
- **Action:** it changes what gets made next.

Weak: "UGC outperforms studio in this niche." Strong: "Gummy buyers don't doubt creatine works; they doubt a gummy holds a real dose. Every comment thread turns to 'how many do I have to eat', so the ad that shows the dose wins trust the taste ads skip."

**Prioritise by restraint.** Lead with the one finding that changes the most and give it the most room; mention the rest in proportion; cut the merely interesting. If everything feels equally important, judgment is missing.

## 5. Concepts from data (Motion concept-standards, Adgence creative-strategy)

A concept = one angle + one mechanic in one format. Hook swaps and edits are variations, not concepts.

**Every concept carries:** title (2 to 5 words, the strategic idea), persona (specific enough to cast), pain or desire in their words, awareness stage, angle, mechanic, format, hook in three layers (first frame, on-screen text, spoken line), the proof the user holds, offer and CTA, **grounded in** (the named data point: "the 'how many gummies' doubt, 38 of 612 comments, no live ad answers it"), **hypothesis** ("If we show the dose in the first 3 s to people who doubt gummies, hook rate beats our baseline and CPA lands under target, because the doubt is what stops them; we'll know after $300 or 7 days"), production difficulty (low, medium, high), and why it can fail.

**Quality bar:** a specific human tension (complete "this exists because [person] struggles with [lived moment]"); all parts reinforce each other; couldn't run for a competitor unchanged; the format chosen on purpose; the hook implicates the viewer; it can clearly win or lose; a team could make it without a meeting.

**Three concepts differ on at least two of:** persona, angle, format, hook tactic. Not three hooks on one UGC talking head.

**Ranking (H):** evidence strength (count, intensity, buyers over browsers) × gap (open beats crowded; owned only with a better proof) × awareness fit for the budget × production ease. Iterate on proven before inventing (Denney): with an account, concept 1 is often a new angle on the format that already wins.

**Anti-patterns:** anything you could write without this brand's data; "do more UGC"; "more of what works" (that's optimisation); copying a competitor's ad; a concept whose proof the user doesn't hold; a claim that needs a medical, weight, income or guarantee promise.

**Learn the mechanism, change the execution** when a concept comes from a competitor or a viral video: different hook, different angle or different visual; never their words, footage or people.

## 6. Budget and stage calibration (Motion; Adgence creative-strategy)

| Monthly spend (H) | New concepts a week | Awareness focus | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under $10K | 2 (top quarter of accounts ship about 5 creatives a week, Motion vendor data) | Problem- and solution-aware: they already know the pain | 2 concepts × 2 text hooks × 2 visual hooks (Sanchez) |
| $10K to $50K | 3 to 4 (top quarter about 8) | Add product-aware proof | 3-2-2 to validate an angle (3 creatives × 2 texts × 2 headlines) |
| $50K+ | 5+ (top quarter 16+) | Add unaware: cold reach needs problem reveal | 3-3-3 to map a new angle (3 concepts × 3 formats × 3 hooks) |
| No budget given | Plan for 2 a week | Problem- and solution-aware | Statics first to validate the angle cheaply (offer-first and text statics win often: Motion 2026 benchmarks, vendor) |

Meta treats look-alike ads (same person, same opening) as one; diversify on persona, angle, format and visual opening, keep structure simple (broad targeting) and refresh every 2 to 4 weeks at scale (practitioners, H). TikTok: 3 to 5 meaningfully different creatives per ad group.

## 7. The 30-day test plan

- **Week 1:** concept 1 (strongest evidence) as 2 to 3 hooks on one body, or as statics to validate the angle; concept 2 if budget allows.
- **Week 2:** read concept 1 (hook rate first, then CTR, then CPA); start concept 2 or 3.
- **Week 3:** iterate the winner in order hooks → body or format → new angle, one variable a round; kill losers.
- **Week 4:** the winner into scaling; the month's learnings into the strategy file; next month's map.

Each test row: concept, what varies, budget share, success measure, kill rule, read date. Defaults (H): read after 7 days or about 2x the target CPA in spend per ad, whichever first; kill an ad at 2x target CPA with no conversion; keep a hook whose hook rate beats the account baseline by 20%+ even if CPA is still noisy, and test it on a new body. Name every ad Concept_Angle_Hook#_Body# so results map back. Without an account, write the measures as rules the user applies in Ads Manager.

## 8. Learnings and memory

- A learning is a claim with its evidence, date and confidence: "Dose-first hooks beat taste hooks on hook rate (own ads, 2 tests, $1,200 spend, Medium)". Market learnings are labelled bets.
- Each weekly read moves a test to **Confirmed**, **Killed** or **Inconclusive** (with why: not enough spend, not a fair test). Inconclusive twice means change the test, not run it again.
- A strategy belief changes only when a learning contradicts it; say what changed and why.
- What's in memory is dated; a figure older than 30 days is restated as "as of {date}" or refreshed.

## 9. Data accuracy (Motion)

- **Read** (from a tool this run, or a dated note in memory): as is, with its source.
- **Worked out:** say how in one line ("41 of 612 = 6.7%").
- **Blank:** say why ("no ad account connected, so CPA is blank"). Never fill a blank with a guess. A wrong answer is worse than a blank.
- Generative output (concepts, hooks, briefs): "Grounded in: [data point]" on each; a direction with no data behind it is labelled a hypothesis to test.
- Every count states its base; every report is dated.

## 10. Voice

Direct, short, specific numbers. Every weakness comes with what to do instead. Don't lead with caveats, don't soften bad news. Orient with options when intent is open ("I see three ways in: …; which first?"), check in at surprising findings, offer specific next steps, match the user's pace.
