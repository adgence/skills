## The call: borrow the structure, not the words

**Why it's still running:** it doesn't sell protein. It sells quitting the $6 cafe run. The hook names a habit the buyer already pays for every day ("Why is everyone dropping St\*rbucks…"), and then every benefit arrives as a *guess*, not a claim: is it the price, the calories, the protein, the cravings, the chalky shakes? "Well, I think we will never know." It reads like gossip, so nobody's sales guard goes up, and the pouch is in every single frame, so even a sound-off scroller learns the product.

**The proof it's their proven message, not a one-off:** Javvy's own page (46 live US ads) has only 3 ads past 90 days (Meta's count), and the **copy** on all three sells the same swap: "Iced coffee that actually keeps you full?" (111 days), "Still spending $6/day on iced coffee that leaves you wired and hungry?" (95 days), "Coffee used to keep me awake, but I'd always get the jitters by noon" (95 days). I haven't watched those three videos, so I can't say they *open* on the cafe habit: their copy does. What that tells you already: the words they keep paying for are **replace the cafe habit**, with protein as the reason to believe, not the headline. And they're still adding copies of this video (2 new ads on the same creative on 20 Nov). Confidence: **High** that the cafe swap is the message they keep paying for (run time, copies, the same copy across their long runners); **Medium** on what their other videos do (unwatched); **MISSING** whether any of it is profitable.

**What to do this week:** shoot one 30 s, one-take, hands-only "why is everyone switching from [the drink your buyer buys]" recipe with your bottle in every frame, three to five of your real benefits as guesses, and a playful non-answer at the end. Cost: a phone and an afternoon.

## Why it works, layer by layer

| Layer | What it does | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| First frame (0 s) | Ice dropping into an empty branded shaker, pouch readable behind it | Hook frames |
| On-screen text (0 to 3.5 s) | "Why is everyone dropping St\*rbucks for this protein coffee?" (the brand name starred out on screen) | Frames |
| Spoken (0 to 3 s) | The same line, a woman's voiceover | Transcript |
| The angle | Habit swap: the cafe drink is the enemy (price and calories), the product is the easier habit | Transcript, 3 sister ads |
| Awareness | Solution-aware: they already buy iced coffee and know protein is good; it tells them which habit to change | Worked out |
| Proof | None stated. The demo is the proof: it shows how easy it is (ice, water, milk, two scoops, shake) | Frames 3.5 to 31 s |
| Offer | Today's cut carries a Black Friday layer: "this sale ends at midnight" sticker and "their Black Friday sale with free gifts" in the voiceover | Frames 3.5 s, 31.5 s |
| CTA | "Shop now" button + the sale sticker; the script itself ends on a joke, not an ask | Copy, frames |

**Pacing (measured):** 34.2 s, 1 detected cut (23.3 s): one continuous take, confirmed on the frames. 95 spoken words, 2.8 a second. Product on screen from 0.0 s and never leaves.

**The four questions** (judged from the creative, not from their numbers, which nobody outside Javvy can see):
1. *Makes sense fast?* Yes. Question + pouch in frame 1: you know it's a protein coffee and that it replaces Starbucks in 2 seconds.
2. *Feels like it's for me?* Yes, if you buy iced coffee. It names the moment (the cafe run), not a demographic.
3. *Believable?* Mostly. Questions dodge the burden of proof. One weak spot: "helps to control appetite and sugar cravings" is a health-style claim they say without backing.
4. *Reason to act?* Only through the sale overlay. Without Black Friday the script has no reason to buy today: that's why they bolt a sale onto it.

## What to copy and what not to

| Copy (structure) | Don't copy |
|---|---|
| Open on the habit your buyer already pays for, as a "why is everyone switching" question | "Starbucks" or any brand name: Javvy stars it out for a reason; naming a competitor in an ad needs proof and legal sign-off |
| Benefits as guesses, ending on a wink | Their exact lines and the "we will never know" close |
| One take, hands only, pack in every frame | "Helps control appetite and sugar cravings" or any body effect you can't back |
| A sale layer on the same footage at sale time | The free-gifts offer unless you really run one |

**Your version (draft, needs your facts):**
- Hook: "Why is everyone quitting their [MISSING: the drink your buyers replace] for this?" over ice going into a glass, your bottle beside it.
- Guesses (only the ones that are true for you): the cost per cup [MISSING: your price per serving vs a cafe drink], the protein per serving [MISSING], no shaker needed (a concentrate's real edge over powder: you pour, they scoop and shake), the taste.
- Close: a playful non-answer, not a claim. CTA from your offer (none if you have none; don't invent a deadline).

## Three tests to run

| Test | Hypothesis | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Habit-swap one-take (above) | Naming the cafe habit stops your buyers better than naming protein | Hook rate (3 s views ÷ impressions) vs your current best |
| 2. Same body, hook "pour vs scoop" (your bottle pouring next to a powder scoop clumping) | Your format edge is the reason to switch from powder brands like Javvy | Hook rate, then CPA |
| 3. Same body with a sale sticker in your next promo | The script needs a dated reason to buy (Javvy's does) | CPA in the promo window vs test 1 |
