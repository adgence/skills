## 1. The call

- **What makes it work:** satisfying process footage *earns* the taste claim. You watch creatine become candy for 9 seconds before anyone says "taste", so you believe it. 14 cuts in 21.9 s keep it moving; the gummy is on screen at 6.5 s.
- **Can you remake it?** Yes, if you can film your own real process (your kitchen test batches, or your coating step at the factory with permission). If a manufacturer makes everything and you can't film there, don't fake a kitchen: shoot the version at the end of section 3 (the flavor table).
- **The test:** does watching the sour coat go on sell "sour" better than saying it? Four hooks on one body.

One thing you should know first: "sour creatine gummies" is not empty ground. A creator sponsored by Arrae posted "try these sour creatine gummies @Arrae" on 3 Oct 2026 (202,355 views). Sour still beats Crave's "candy", but you're counter-positioning against Arrae too, so the *making* of the sour is what should be yours.

## 3. The brief

**Concept:** `SourMakingOf_Taste_H1-4_FacelessBroll`
**Hypothesis:** buyers who find gummies "too sweet" or powder "chalky" respond to seeing the sour coat being made more than to being told it's sour.
**Who shoots it:** you, on your phone, overhead where possible. Gloves, plain counter, no other brand's equipment logos turned to camera.
**Angle:** taste (sour) · **Mechanic:** process reveal, show don't tell · **Awareness:** solution-aware (they know creatine; they hate how they take it).

### Do not say (and why)
| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "Candy-like", "took over a year", "flavor after flavor, batch after batch", "no bitter aftertaste, no chalkiness", "you might as well enjoy it" | Crave's words. Borrow the structure, never the lines |

### If you can't film your own making
Same 15-shot rhythm at a **flavor table**: unlabelled test cups of coating, you tasting and rejecting two, the third makes you wince, then the pouch. It keeps "show, don't tell" without implying you cook it in your kitchen.

## 4. How to judge it

- **Hook rate** (3-second views ÷ impressions) decides between H1 to H4. Give each the same budget and at least a few thousand impressions before reading it.
- **Hold rate** to 50% judges the body: does the coating shot keep people?
- **CPA** against your account average decides the concept.
- **Diagnosis:** weak hook rate everywhere → the process opening isn't the problem, try H4's reaction as the first frame for all. Strong hook, weak CPA → the body doesn't pay off "sour": move the wince earlier.
- **What we learn even if it loses:** whether showing the sour sells better than saying it, and which of taste, chalk or consistency is your opener.
